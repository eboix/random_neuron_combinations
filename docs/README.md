# Experiment writeups

The question behind everything in this repo: are single neurons more interpretable than random linear combinations of
neurons, and if so, why? Two studies measure the gap: the vision **microscope** (CLIP coherence and class purity of
the top images, top-level `README.md`) and the LM **agent study** (`combo_interp/`, pilot writeup in
`combo_interp/docs/pilot_writeup.tex`). The two experiments below then test one explanation for it: the neuron
specialization mechanism of Abedsoltan et al., *Revenge of Monosemanticity* (arXiv 2608.24007).

* **Experiment A (centroids).** Do the neurons' input weights partition the MLP's inputs better than random sets of
  directions? This tests whether the weights are aligned with structure in the data.
* **Experiment B (locality).** Are a unit's strongest firings close together in MLP-input space, and does that
  explain the neuron-vs-combination gap? This tests the "a combination fires in a union of regions" mechanism.

## Results in brief

**Experiment A, Qwen3-0.6B.**
* **Max inner product** (the main analysis): each token goes to the neuron with the largest gate pre-activation. With
  all 3072 neurons, the gate rows' partition explains 1.3–2.0× the input variance of the best random set. It beats
  random directions with the gate rows' own mean and covariance in all 12 (layer, M) cells by median.
* **Cosine assignment:** gate rows beat their randomly rotated copies everywhere, but they are not centres of the
  input's main clusters (random tokens are much better centroids).
* **Both rules:** mixing neurons erodes the advantage, and an untrained model shows none of it.

**Experiment B, Qwen3-0.6B.**
* **Locality predicts explainability:** within a condition, units with more local top firings get higher simulator
  scores (ρ = 0.53).
* **It explains part of the gap:** locality accounts for about a third to 60 % of the neurons' `sim_spearman` lead
  over random combinations.
* **Small sparse combinations act like neurons:** their top firings come from one constituent neuron.
* **`related_20` is unexplained:** it is very local yet still scores worse than neurons.
* **Per-neuron weight–centroid alignment is a selection effect:** a random direction aligns with its own top firings
  just as much.

**ConvNeXt-tiny (A and B).**
* **Exact reproduction:** the published units' top images reproduce exactly, and CLIP coherence matches the published
  values (r = 0.985).
* **Experiment A:** with max inner product, `pwconv1` rows beat every null, including the covariance-matched one, in
  the mid block at every M. In the last block they win with enough centroids. With all 3072 neurons (cosine rule), their partition predicts the
  ImageNet class better than k-means.
* **Experiment B:** locality accounts for about 80 % of the CLIP-coherence gap of dense random combinations.

Note on centering: the Qwen max-inner-product writeup treats uncentered inputs (the network's own pre-activations) as
primary. The ConvNeXt writeup's main max-inner-product table uses centered inputs, and its uncentered results are in
the same folder (`centroids_cos_ip/summary_raw.md`).
